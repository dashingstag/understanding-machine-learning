Scaling laws

MapPlaybackNotes

Empirical power laws linking loss to compute, parameters and data — Chinchilla and what survives of it.

Statement

Empirical power laws: test loss-l falls as a smooth power of n-params $N$, data-d $D$ and compute-c $C$ — $L(N,D) = E + A/N^{\alpha} + B/D^{\beta}$ with $\alpha,\beta \approx 0.3$ (Kaplan et al. 2020; Hoffmann et al. 2022). The chinchilla result: at fixed compute, train compute-optimally at $D \approx 20N$ — most models were under-trained, not too small.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

modern-machine-learning — scaling laws are how labs turn budget into architecture: they predict the final loss of a $10^{25}$-FLOP run from small-scale extrapolation, so the billion-dollar decision is made on a curve, not a hunch. They're the field's main forecasting instrument and the premise behind emergent-abilities debates.

Teardown

  1. The observation. On log–log axes, loss vs each factor is linear over 6+ orders of magnitude — kaplan-laws.
  2. Chinchilla. Fixed $C$: loss is U-shaped in $N$ — too big wastes FLOPs on params, too small wastes them on data. Optimum: $N^* \propto C^{0.5}$, $D^* \propto C^{0.5}$, ratio ≈ 20 tokens/param.
  3. Predictive use. Fit exponents at $10^{18}$–$10^{21}$ FLOPs, extrapolate to $10^{25}$ — Muennighoff & Hoffmann style; modern labs report it works within ~2%.
  4. What they miss. Capabilities (not loss) can appear discontinuously — emergent-abilities — and downstream tasks correlate imperfectly (evaluation, goodhart-law).
  5. The other axis. reasoning-models add test-time compute: loss vs inference FLOPs also obeys law-like curves — the post-2024 scaling frontier.
  6. Data limits. Repeated-epoch scaling decays (Muennighoff et al., arXiv:2305.17493) — fresh training-data is the binding constraint.

Worked example

Chinchilla sizing: budget $C = 6\times10^{23}$ FLOPs.

  • $6ND = C$ and $D = 20N$ → $120N^2 = C$ → $N^* = \sqrt{5\times10^{21}} \approx 7.1\times10^{10}$ → ~70B params, ~1.4T tokens — the recipe LLaMA-65B later validated.

Open questions

  • Why power laws at all — which property of data/model makes the exponents universal? — an open theory problem.
  • Does the curve survive the shift to test-time compute and synthetic data, or does the field need new laws? — reasoning-models, training-data.

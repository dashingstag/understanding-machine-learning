Verifiable rewards

MapNotes

RL against checkable answers — math, code, unit tests — the training signal behind the reasoning-model era.

Statement

RL where the reward comes from a checker, not a learned model: exact- match math answers, passing unit tests, formal proof acceptance, game outcomes. reward-r becomes ground truth — unhackable, dense, and cheap — and rlhf-style optimization (usually GRPO) can push pi-policy hard without goodhart-law biting.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

reasoning-models — long chain-of-thought is only improvable where correctness is cheap to verify. Verifiability is the enabling condition of the whole test-time-compute era and the boundary it currently stops at.

Teardown

  1. The checker. Math: exact/graded match (GSM, MATH); code: unit tests + hidden tests; formal: Lean/Coq acceptance — binary or graded signal, no model in the loop.
  2. Why it resists hacking. A reward model's blind spots don't exist when the reward is a test — the optimization target is the real thing, so rlhf's over-optimization problem mostly evaporates.
  3. GRPO. Group-relative advantage: sample $k$ solutions per problem, normalize reward-r within the group — no value network needed; DeepSeek-R1's engine.
  4. Process vs outcome. Outcome reward (final answer only) is cheap; process rewards (per-step scores, Lightman et al.) give denser signal but reintroduce a learned, hackable scorer.
  5. The frontier problem. Most valuable domains (essays, strategy, science) lack checkers — the field is extending verification via rubric judges, self-consistency, and learned critics.

Worked example

Math problem, $k = 8$ sampled solutions, GRPO:

  • 5 correct (reward 1), 3 wrong (reward 0) → advantages: correct $+0.6$, wrong $-1.0$ after group normalization.
  • The policy is pushed toward whatever the 5 did — including a 12-step chain with a self-check nobody trained. That trace behaviour is discovered by the reward shape, which is the deep point.

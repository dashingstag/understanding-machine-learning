Latent diffusion

MapNotes

Diffusing in a VAE latent space instead of pixels — the step that made high-resolution generation cheap.

Statement

Run diffusion-models' denoising in a learned compressed space instead of pixel space: a VAE encodes 512² RGB to a ~64×64 latent (perceptual compression ~8× per side), the noise-prediction network operates there, and the decoder renders once at the end. Rombach et al. 2022 — the compute trick that put image generation on consumer GPUs.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

diffusion-models — pixel-space DDPM needed 1000+ expensive steps on full-resolution tensors; the latent move is the reason Stable Diffusion is cheap and open. Video, audio and 3-D generators all reuse it.

Teardown

  1. The autoencoder. Encoder: pixels → smooth low-res latent; decoder: latent → pixels. Trained perceptually (LPIPS + adversarial) so the latent carries semantics, not noise.
  2. Diffusion in latent. ddpm-loss unchanged — but each UNet/DiT pass is ~50× cheaper; sampling 20–50 steps is seconds.
  3. Why it preserves quality. Compression removes imperceptible high-frequency redundancy — the denoiser spends capacity on structure, not dithering.
  4. Conditioning. Cross-attention layers read text embed-e (CLIP/T5) — how "a photo of X" steers the latent trajectory.
  5. Failure modes. Latent ceiling: fine text/faces degrade through the VAE; higher-ratio autoencoders trade fidelity for speed.

Worked example

512×512×3 = 786k values vs 64×64×4 = 16k latent values — a 48× reduction. One denoising step in latent ≈ 2% of the pixel-space cost — 50 steps ≈ 1 pixel step's worth of work, which is the entire consumer story.

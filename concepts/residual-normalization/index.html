Residuals and normalization

MapPlaybackNotes

The residual stream plus layer/RMS norm — the plumbing that makes 100-layer stacks trainable and legible.

Statement

Every transformer sublayer is wrapped as $x \mapsto x + \mathrm{sublayer}(\mathrm{Norm}(x))$: the skip connection keeps the residual stream intact, and layernorm rescales each position's vector to zero-mean/unit-variance (RMSNorm drops the mean subtraction). Together they keep gradients finite and signal readable through 100+ layers — see transformer-block for where they sit.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

transformer-architecture — without residual paths, a 96-layer stack's gradient vanishes long before layer 1 (gradient-descent stalls); without norms, activations drift and LR tuning becomes impossible. This node is why depth is cheap, and why interpretability can read the stream.

Teardown

  1. The skip. $x + f(x)$ gives every layer a direct identity path — gradients flow backward unimpeded; the sublayer learns a delta, not a replacement. (Residual learning: He et al. 2015, ResNet.)
  2. The stream. Residuals make the hidden state a shared bus: every sublayer reads it and writes back — the object interpretability reads, activation-steering edits.
  3. The norm. layernorm = $(x-\mu)/\sigma \cdot \gamma + \beta$ per position. RMSNorm (Llama): $x/\mathrm{RMS}(x)\cdot g$, no mean — ~10% cheaper, same effect.
  4. Pre vs post. Original: post-norm $x + \mathrm{Norm}(f(x))$ — fragile at depth. Modern: pre-norm — norm inside the branch, identity path pure — what GPT/Llama use.
  5. Why not BatchNorm. Sequences have variable length and tiny effective batches — batch statistics are unstable; per-position normalization has no batch dependence.

Worked example

Signal magnitude check: without residual, a 96-layer net with per-layer gain 1.05 sees forward scale $1.05^{96} \approx 108\times$ — and gradients shrink by the same factor backward. With the skip path, the gradient at layer 1 includes a copy of the output gradient directly — the identity term never multiplies below 1.

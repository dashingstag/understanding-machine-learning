Gradient descent

MapPlaybackNotes

Iterative parameter updates along the negative gradient — the optimizer family every model in this map trains with.

gd-update

Statement

Iteratively update the parameters by stepping against the loss gradient:

$$\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\,\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(\theta)$$

$\mathcal{L}$ is the scalar loss-l, $\eta$ the learning-rate, and $\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}$ is produced by backpropagation. Repeated over batches of training-data, this is the learning rule — every model in this map was produced by some variant of it (gd-update).

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

ml-foundations claims "learning is optimization"; this node is the optimization. Nothing else on the map works without it — pretraining is gradient descent for $10^{13}$ tokens, RLHF is gradient descent on a different loss, and the fine-tuning inside every product is the same loop on a small dataset.

Teardown

  1. The gradient. For each parameter, $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial \theta_i$ answers "if I nudge this weight up, does the loss rise or fall?" — computed once per step by backpropagation.
  2. The step. $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\nabla\mathcal{L}$. Steepest local descent; no lookahead, no momentum — yet.
  3. Batch → stochastic. Full-batch gradients are exact but waste the dataset's redundancy. Minibatch (SGD) is noisier per step and far cheaper per epoch — and the noise itself helps escape bad basins.
  4. The learning rate. Too small → nothing converges in budget; too large → oscillation/divergence. Practice: short warmup, then decay (cosine/linear). LR is the single most-tuned hyperparameter.
  5. Momentum and adaptivity. Accumulate velocity (Polyak 1964, Nesterov) and per-parameter step sizes (AdaGrad → RMSProp → Adam, Kingma & Ba 2014). Modern runs default to Adam-family — torn down in optimizers.
  6. Where it fails. Sharp minima vs flat minima (generalization), saddle points, gradient pathologies (vanishing/exploding) that residual-normalization exists to fix.

Worked example

Minimize $\mathcal{L}(w) = (w - 3)^2$ from $w_0 = 0$:

  • $\nabla = 2(w - 3)$; at $w = 0$: $\nabla = -6$.
  • $\eta = 0.25$: $w_1 = 0 - 0.25 \cdot (-6) = 1.5$; then $w_2 = 1.5 - 0.25 \cdot (-3) = 2.25$; converges to 3 geometrically.
  • $\eta = 1.5$: $w_1 = 0 - 1.5 \cdot (-6) = 9$; next $\nabla = 12$, $w_2 = 9 - 18 = -9$ — oscillation doubling away from 3: divergence from one oversized step.
  • $\eta = 0.05$: safe but $\sim 40$ steps to the same answer — the speed/stability trade in two lines.

MapNotes

The scratchpad trace itself — the substrate reasoning models are trained to produce, extend and improve.

Statement

Generating intermediate reasoning steps as tokens before the final answer — "let's think step by step". Functionally it's working memory: a transformer can't store intermediate products between layers, but it can write them as tokens and read them back through attention-mechanism. The substrate reasoning-models are trained to produce, extend and improve.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

reasoning-models — test-time compute only exists because the model can spend tokens on intermediate steps; the chain is the currency that axis spends. Also the least-trained capability in the stack: discovered by prompting, industrialized by RL.

Teardown

  1. The mechanism. Each generated token re-enters the context — self-produced working memory; the KV cache becomes the scratchpad.
  2. Prompting discovery. Wei et al. 2022: few-shot traces → arithmetic jumps ~3×; zero-shot "step by step" (Kojima et al.) showed it's latent, not learned from the examples.
  3. Self-consistency. Wang et al.: sample k chains, majority-vote — cheap ensembling over reasoning paths.
  4. Faithfulness problem. Turpin et al. 2023: traces can post-rationalize — the written chain isn't guaranteed to be the real computation (relevant to interpretability).
  5. From prompt to policy. reasoning-models bake CoT in via RL — long hidden traces, backtracking, verification emerge as trained behaviour, not prompt tricks.

Worked example

"A café has 23 apples, buys 2 crates of 12, uses 20."

  • Direct: often wrong — one forward pass can't hold $23 + 24 - 20$'s intermediate.
  • CoT: "23 + 24 = 47; 47 − 20 = 27" — each product is written to the context where the next step can attend to it. The chain isn't explanation; it's storage.

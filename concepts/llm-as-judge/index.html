LLM-as-judge

MapNotes

Using a model to grade a model — the scalable but biased evaluator modern evals lean on.

Statement

Using a language model to grade another model's outputs — pairwise preference, rubric scores, or pass/fail — where no answer key exists. The scalable evaluator evaluation leans on for open-ended tasks (chat quality, agent trajectories, safety refusals), with documented biases: verbosity, position, self-preference, format favouritism.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

evaluation — most valuable behaviours can't be checked by string match; a judge model is how they get graded at all. Its biases set the noise floor of every open-ended benchmark result and are a goodhart-law surface: models get tuned to please the judge.

Teardown

  1. Modes. Pairwise (which answer is better — position-swapped to cancel order bias), rubric score (1–10 on defined criteria), and G-Eval-style (grade against a checklist of steps).
  2. Known biases. Position bias (first/second swap changes verdicts), verbosity bias (longer wins), self-preference (judges favour their own family), and authority mimicry (confident tone beats correct hedging).
  3. Calibration. Judge vs human agreement is the reported metric — ~80% on MT-Bench-class tasks; lower on technical correctness where the judge can't verify claims.
  4. Judge drift. Same judge, different versions → different rankings — results are only comparable under a pinned judge, prompt and protocol (the reproducibility requirement).
  5. Meta-risk. Training against the judge bakes its taste into the model — eval-judge and training-judge circularity is a real contamination-adjacent worry.

Worked example

MT-Bench-style pairwise grade: answer A (concise, correct) vs B (verbose, mostly correct):

  • Judge picks B ~60% of the time — verbosity bias measured directly by shuffling order: if verdicts flip, the judge read length, not quality. Position swap + rubric anchoring is the standard mitigation.

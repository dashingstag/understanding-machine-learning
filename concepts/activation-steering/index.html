Activation steering

MapNotes

Editing activations mid-forward-pass to steer behaviour — the use-the-parts-you-found half of interpretability.

Statement

Edit behaviour by adding directions to the residual stream mid- forward-pass: compute a steering vector (e.g., mean activation difference between "refuse" and "comply" examples), add a scaled copy to embed-e-space activations during generation, and the behaviour shifts — no gradient-descent, no retraining. The use-the-parts-you- found half of interpretability.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

interpretability — it's the causal test of everything the field claims: if adding a direction reliably changes behaviour, the feature was real. It's also the cheapest possible intervention — inference- time control without touching weights-theta.

Teardown

  1. Finding the direction. Mean-difference of activations between contrast pairs ("honest" vs "sycophantic" completions), or read off an sparse-autoencoders feature directly.
  2. Applying it. $\mathbf{h} \leftarrow \mathbf{h} + \alpha \mathbf{v}$ at chosen layers per token — strength α controls the dosage; too large → gibberish.
  3. Golden Gate demo. Anthropic's mascot experiment: steer with a bridge-feature direction and the model answers everything about the Golden Gate — proof the direction carries meaning.
  4. Honesty/safety uses. Honesty vectors (turn down sycophancy), deception probes, refusal directions — alignment knobs you can dial at inference.
  5. Limits. Coarse control (real traits aren't single directions), context sensitivity (a "honesty" vector can break syntax), and the open question of whether it edits causes or decorates them.

Worked example

Sycophancy steering (illustrative): collect 100 prompt pairs where the model agrees-with-user vs disagrees-honestly; direction = mean(honest) − mean(agreeing) at layer 15; $\alpha = -3$ during decode → flattery rate drops measurably on a held-out test — control with zero weight updates.

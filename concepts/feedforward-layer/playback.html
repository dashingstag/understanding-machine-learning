The other half of the block

Did you know?

The FFN holds ~⅔ of a transformer's parameters but gets ~5% of the intuition — most "transformer explained" diagrams shrink it to an unlabeled box.

In the real world

Mixture of experts is literally this layer, replicated with a router — everything MoE does is an FFN trick.

Per block: attention mixes across positions, then the FFN transforms each position alone — same weights everywhere, $\mathrm{FFN}(x) = W_2\,\sigma(W_1 x)$, with inner width $4d$.

Where the knowledge lives

Did you know?

Geva et al. (2021) read FFN layers as key–value memories: first matmul's columns are pattern keys, second's rows are values. "Eiffel Tower → Paris" fires specific mid-layer neurons.

In the real world

Model-editing methods (ROME, MEMIT) try to rewrite single facts inside these matrices — surgical knowledge surgery, still imperfect. See interpretability.

Attention decides what's relevant now; the FFN is where stored facts, patterns and features get applied. That's why probes and SAEs spend so much time staring at these layers.

The nonlinearity in the middle

Did you know?

Noam Shazeer's GLU paper turned the FFN into a gated unit — SwiGLU won the bake-off and now lives in Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen… and MoE routers.

Without $\sigma$ the two matmuls collapse into one — the nonlinearity is the whole reason for expanding to $4d$. ReLU → GELU → SiLU/SwiGLU: each generation kept the shape, swapped the squiggle.

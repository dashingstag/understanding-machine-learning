Optimizers (SGD, momentum, Adam)

MapNotes

What actually performs the update: momentum, adaptive learning rates, weight decay — and why Adam won.

Statement

The update-rule family applied to gradient-descent's output: SGD adds momentum (velocity $v \leftarrow \beta v + \nabla\mathcal{L}$, step by $v$); Adam (Kingma & Ba 2014) tracks both gradient mean and variance ($m, v$) and steps by $\hat m/(\sqrt{\hat v}+\epsilon)$ — per-parameter adaptive rates. AdamW decouples weight decay; most frontier training is Adam-family at learning-rate $10^{-4}$ scale with warmup+cosine decay.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

ml-foundations — the descent rule is a choice with real consequences: Adam's per-parameter normalization is why transformers train stably at scale where SGD stalls, and its hyperparameters ($\beta_1, \beta_2$, beta weight decay) fill every run sheet.

Teardown

  1. SGD. Pure gd-update on minibatches — simple, generalizes well, but fragile: one global LR for 10¹¹ heterogeneous parameters.
  2. Momentum. Polyak/Nesterov velocity — smooths noise, accelerates through valleys, overshoots less; still one LR.
  3. AdaGrad → RMSProp. Per-parameter step sizes scaled by accumulated squared gradients — rare/large gradients get smaller steps.
  4. Adam. Momentum ($m$, β₁=0.9) + RMSProp ($v$, β₂=0.999) + bias correction — the 2015 default; memory cost: 2 extra tensors per parameter (a real line-item at 100B+, cf. distributed-training).
  5. AdamW. Weight decay pulled out of the gradient path — the actual standard; variants (Lion, Sophia, Muon) challenge the crown at frontier scale.
  6. Schedules. Linear warmup (first ~0.5% steps), then cosine/linear decay — learning-rate is the highest-leverage knob.

Worked example

Parameter with gradients $[10, 0.1, 10, 0.1, \dots]$ (alternating scale):

  • SGD, $\eta$: oscillates — 10s dominate the step.
  • Momentum: direction stabilizes, oscillation damped by β.
  • Adam: each step ≈ $\eta \cdot \text{sign-like}$ — the 10s and 0.1s get equal effective step size via $\sqrt{\hat v}$ normalization: the noisy parameter converges without a global LR compromise.

Open questions

  • Second-order methods (Shampoo, Muon) at frontier scale — can they beat AdamW's generality without its tuning tax?
  • Why does Adam outperform SGD on transformers specifically — curvature, sparsity, or noise structure? — still debated.

Positional encoding

MapPlaybackNotes

Injecting order into a permutation-blind architecture — sinusoids, learned positions, RoPE and its variants.

Statement

Self-attention is permutation-invariant: shuffle the input and every output row shuffles with it — nothing marks "first" or "after". Positional encoding injects order, either by adding position vectors to embed-e (sinusoidal/learned, Vaswani et al. 2017) or by rotating query-matrix/key-matrix pairs as a function of position (RoPE, Su et al. 2021), which makes attention scores depend on relative distance.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

transformer-architecture — without position, the stack is a bag-of-words machine: "dog bites man" ≡ "man bites dog". RoPE specifically is why modern models can extend context (YaRN, NTK scaling) — the encoding family decides length-extrapolation behaviour.

Teardown

  1. The problem. Attention compares all pairs symmetrically; order must enter as data, not structure.
  2. Absolute methods. Sinusoidal $PE_{(pos,2i)} = \sin(pos/10000^{2i/d})$ — fixed, frequency-spaced; learned position embeddings — trainable but capped at train-time max length.
  3. Relative methods. What attention wants is distance $m-n$, not addresses. RoPE rotates each 2-D slice of $\mathbf{q}_m,\mathbf{k}_n$ by angle $m\theta_i$, so $\mathbf{q}_m^\top\mathbf{k}_n$ becomes a function of $(m-n)$ — free, parameterless, and composable with attention-mechanism unchanged.
  4. Length extrapolation. Models trained at 4k fail at 100k — frequencies hit unseen phases. Fixes: position interpolation, NTK/YaRN frequency rescaling — the engineering behind "1M context" claims (inference-efficiency).

Worked example

RoPE on a 2-D pair $(q_1, q_2)$ at position $m$: rotation matrix $R_{m\theta}$ maps $q \mapsto (q_1\cos m\theta - q_2\sin m\theta,\; q_1\sin m\theta + q_2\cos m\theta)$. Then $\mathbf{q}_m^\top \mathbf{k}_n = \mathbf{q}^\top R_{(m-n)\theta}\,\mathbf{k}$ — depends only on the offset. With $\theta = 1$: tokens 5 apart always produce the same relative rotation regardless of where in the sequence they sit.

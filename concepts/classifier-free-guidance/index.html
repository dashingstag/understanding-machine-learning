Classifier-free guidance

MapNotes

Amplifying prompt adherence by extrapolating away from an unconditional pass — the guidance-scale knob.

Statement

Steer diffusion-models sampling toward the prompt by running the denoiser twice — conditioned $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c)$ and unconditioned $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)$ — and extrapolating: $\hat\epsilon = \epsilon_{uncond} + s\,(\epsilon_{cond} - \epsilon_{uncond})$. The guidance scale $s$ (typically 1.5–12) is the dial every prompt UI exposes: higher = prompt-faithful, lower = freer.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

diffusion-models — text conditioning alone produces weak adherence; CFG is what makes prompts actually bind. It's also the source of diffusion's signature failure modes (saturated colors, artefacts at high $s$) — knowing what the knob does explains what it breaks.

Teardown

  1. Two passes. Same network, run once with conditioning dropped (~10% of training examples drop the condition so the model learns both) — no separate classifier needed.
  2. The extrapolation. Difference $(\epsilon_{cond} - \epsilon_{uncond})$ points "toward the prompt"; scaling by $s>1$ amplifies it — literally walking further in the prompt direction.
  3. The tradeoff. $s$ high → sharp adherence, distorted distribution, artefacts and desaturation; $s$ low → diverse, natural, loosely-on-prompt. Negative $s$ explores the opposite.
  4. Modern patches. Guidance rescale, interval guidance (apply only at certain $t$), distilled CFG (bake guidance in, halve the cost) — the production refinements.

Worked example

$s = 7.5$ (the SD default): the noise estimate is pushed 7.5× in the conditional direction — equivalent to saying "the prompt matters 7.5 times more than the unconditional prior". At $s = 1$: plain conditional sampling; $s = 0$: ignore the prompt entirely.

Expert routing

MapNotes

The gating function that picks experts per token — top-k selection, load balancing, capacity factors.

Statement

The mechanism choosing which experts see each token: a small linear layer produces gate-g scores over $E$ experts, softmax normalizes, top-$k$ winners run and their outputs mix weighted by the gate. Making that discrete choice differentiable — gate scores act as output weights — plus keeping the load balanced is the entire difficulty of mixture-of-experts.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

mixture-of-experts — every MoE paper is mostly a routing paper: the expert FFNs are copies of a known quantity; the router decides whether sparsity is a win or a collapse onto one expert.

Teardown

  1. Top-k gating. Scores → softmax → keep top-$k$ (Switch: $k{=}1$; Mixtral: $k{=}2$; DeepSeek: $k{=}8$ of 256) — output is the gate-weighted sum of expert outputs; gradients flow via the weights.
  2. Load balancing. Without intervention tokens pile onto early winners (rich-get-richer). Fixes: auxiliary load-balance loss (Switch), expert-choice routing, aux-loss-free bias adjustment (DeepSeek-V3's scheme).
  3. Capacity factor. Per-expert token cap $\times$ buffer: overflow tokens are dropped (residual passthrough) — a silent quality leak.
  4. Shared/fine-grained experts. Always-on shared expert handles common structure (DeepSeek); many small experts give finer specialization — current design frontier.
  5. Sequence vs token routing. AuxFree/stable routing, hashing, and learned "expert domains" — alternatives when learned routing oscillates.

Worked example

8 experts, gate logits $[2.1, 0.3, -0.5, 1.8, 0.1, -1, 0.7, 0.2]$, $k=2$:

  • Top-2: experts 0 and 3 → softmax over just those: weights $\approx [0.57, 0.43]$ — output $= 0.57\,E_0(x) + 0.43\,E_3(x)$.
  • If every token picks expert 0, its capacity overflows while experts 1–7 idle: the aux loss punishes exactly this skew.

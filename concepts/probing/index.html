Probing

MapNotes

Lightweight classifiers trained on activations — the cheapest way to ask what a layer knows.

Statement

Train a lightweight classifier (usually linear, sometimes a small MLP) on a frozen model's activations to predict a property — POS, tense, sentiment, truth-value. If the probe reads it, the representation contains it. The cheapest instrument in interpretability — and the oldest, with the deepest caveat: presence ≠ use.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

interpretability — probing is the first thing you point at a new representation: "does layer N encode X?" is answerable in an hour. Its limitations (correlation vs causation, probe learning the task itself) defined what stronger methods had to prove.

Teardown

  1. The setup. Freeze the model, collect hidden states at a layer, train a linear head to predict the labelled property — accuracy = "information is present".
  2. Control tasks. Hewitt & Liang: probe accuracy must beat accuracy on randomized task — otherwise the probe learned the property itself, not the representation's.
  3. Linear vs expressive. Linear probes claim "linearly accessible" information (the strong claim); MLP probes find more but prove less.
  4. MDL probes. Minimum description length / information-theoretic variants — how easily the information is extracted, not just whether.
  5. What probes can't say. Correlation, not causation: the layer may encode X without the network using X — activation-steering and ablation are the causal instruments that complete the claim.

Worked example

Layer-wise POS probing on BERT: accuracy peaks mid-stack (~layers 5–8) then drops — "syntax lives in the middle, semantics at the top" — the canonical layered-representation result (Tenney et al., "BERT rediscovers the classical NLP pipeline").

Open questions

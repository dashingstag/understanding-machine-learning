Evaluation

MapPlaybackNotes

Benchmarks, LLM-as-judge, contamination, saturation and eval-driven development.

Statement

The science of measuring what models can do: curated benchmarks (MMLU, GSM, SWE-bench), human preference arenas (Chatbot Arena), llm-as-judge graders, and perplexity-s on held-out data — undermined by benchmark-contamination (test data in pretraining corpora), saturation, and goodhart-law pressure on every metric that becomes a target.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

modern-machine-learning — every claim in this map ("reasoning works", "MoE wins", "R1 beats o1") stands or falls on evals. Eval methodology is where the field is weakest: contaminated benchmarks, judge bias, and saturation mean headline numbers are often the noisiest signal in the whole system.

Teardown

  1. Intrinsic vs extrinsic. perplexity-s on held-out text vs task accuracy — perplexity predicts pretraining progress; tasks claim capability. They correlate loosely.
  2. Static benchmarks. MMLU (knowledge), GSM8K/MATH (reasoning), HumanEval/SWE-bench (code), ARC/HellaSwag — the canonical suite, all aging and saturating.
  3. Human evaluation. Arena-style pairwise battles → Elo — the gold standard for "feels better", slow and taste-laden.
  4. LLM-as-judge. llm-as-judge: a model grades the model — scalable, but biased (verbosity, position, self-preference).
  5. The contamination problem. benchmark-contamination: test sets scraped into training-data — scores measure memorization, not capability.
  6. Saturation + Goodhart. Benchmarks saturate in ~2 years now — and once a metric is marketed, it gets optimized (goodhart-law); the response is private/live evals and contamination audits.

Worked example

MMLU history as an eval obituary: introduced 2020 with GPT-3 at ~44%; frontier models hit ~90% by 2024 → MMLU-Pro and GPQA minted to restore headroom. The cycle — benchmark → saturation → scandal over contamination → harder successor — is now ~2 years per generation.

Open questions

  • Evals that measure what matters (robustness, honesty, real-world value) vs what's easy to score — the field's unresolved central question.

Pretraining

MapPlaybackNotes

Next-token prediction and masked objectives over trillion-token corpora: data curation, stability, what is actually learned.

Statement

Self-supervised training of a transformer-architecture on training-data at trillion-token scale with the next-token-prediction objective — one cross-entropy term per position. The run consumes compute-c FLOPs, produces the base model every later stage (post-training, knowledge-distillation) assumes, and is where scaling-laws do their forecasting.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

modern-machine-learning — pretraining is where almost all capability enters: post-training shapes behaviour but the knowledge, grammar and latent reasoning are installed here. It's also ~95% of the training compute bill — the stage distributed-training exists to run.

Teardown

  1. Objective. next-token-prediction — cross-entropy on the model's logits-z at every position; perplexity-s is its readable form (ppl).
  2. Data. training-data — curation, dedup, filtering, mixing ratios; half the capability story is corpus design.
  3. Compute. flops-6nd — a full pass ≈ $6ND$ FLOPs for n-params $N$, tokens $D$; at $N = 10^{11}$, $D = 10^{13}$ that's $6\times10^{24}$ — weeks on thousands of GPUs (distributed-training).
  4. Stability. Loss spikes, gradient explosions, hardware failures — the run is babysat via learning-rate schedules, checkpointing, gradient clipping.
  5. What emerges. Grammar first, then facts, then patterns researchers call emergent-abilities — capabilities that appear abruptly with scale (scaling-laws frames the debate).

Worked example

Chinchilla-optimal sizing: chinchilla says $D \approx 20N$ tokens per parameter. For $N = 7$B: $D \approx 140$B tokens.

  • FLOPs ≈ $6 \cdot 7{\times}10^{9} \cdot 1.4{\times}10^{11} \approx 5.9\times10^{21}$.
  • On 1,000 A100s at ~40% MFU (~120 TFLOP/s each): ≈ $1.6\times10^{7}$ s ≈ 190 GPU·days → ~6 days wall-clock.
  • GPT-2-scale check ($N{=}124$M): optimal $D \approx 2.5$B tokens — tiny; the field overshoots (trains longer) for inference economics.

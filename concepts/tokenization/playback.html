Why not characters, why not words

Did you know?

Character models see ~4× longer sequences — and attention is quadratic in length, so characters cost ~16× the mixing compute. Word vocabularies can't spell unseen names at all.

In the real world

Every API price is per token. GPT-class models: ~0.75 words per token in English — and ~2–4× worse compression for low-resource languages, a real cost asymmetry.

Subword units split the difference: frequent strings ("the", "ing") are single tokens; rare ones compose from pieces. The model's vocabulary becomes a learned compression of its corpus — typically 32k–256k entries.

How the vocabulary is built

Did you know?

BPE is a 1994 file-compression algorithm. Sennrich et al. repurposed it for translation in 2016 and it quietly became the alphabet of every frontier LLM.

Byte-pair encoding: start with 256 bytes; repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair; stop at target vocab size. The merge list plus the base alphabet is the entire tokenizer — the panel runs it on a toy string.

Where tokens leak into behaviour

Did you know?

"How many r's in strawberry?" failed because models see Str aw berry — no letters exist at that boundary. Many famous LLM bugs are tokenizer artefacts, not reasoning failures.

In the real world

Chat markup (<|im_start|>), tool calls and reasoning traces are reserved tokens — the tokenizer is also the product's control-channel. See SFT and tool calling.

Numbers split mid-digit, JSON fragments awkwardly, code indents inconsistently. Token boundaries decide what next-token prediction must predict — and what it can never see.

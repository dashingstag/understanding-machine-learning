Words → vectors

Did you know?

Tomas Mikolov's word2vec made headlines in 2013 with a subtraction: king − man + woman landed next to queen — proof the space encoded meaning, not just position.

A one-hot id knows nothing — 'cat' and 'dog' are as distant as 'cat' and 'granite'. A dense embedding vector fixes that: $|\mathcal{V}|$ rows of learned floats where similarity is geometric. The table is just model parameters, learned by the same update as everything else.

The geometry

In the real world

Semantic search, recommendations and every RAG index are this slide in production: embed everything once, then vector search answers "what's near this?" in milliseconds.

Distance answers "are these related?"; direction answers "how?" — gender, tense, scale, sentiment survive as axes you can walk along. The panel animates the classic walk: interpolating between two words passes through their neighbours.

Where they come from

Did you know?

Independently trained models learn aligned spaces — "the platonic representation hypothesis" (Huh et al. 2024): same world, same geometry, different weights.

Three manufacturing routes: (a) predictive — word2vec's "guess the neighbour"; (b) contrastive — CLIP's "pull matching image/text pairs together"; (c) in place — an LLM's input table, trained end to end inside the transformer and usually tied to the output unembedding, so logits are literally similarity scores.

Same math, other modalities

Did you know?

Embeddings are one of the few weight matrices with legible rows: nearest-neighbour lists are human-readable — the original interpretability tool, free with the model.

In the real world

A Spotify song vector, a CLIP image vector and a product vector are the same object on different data — multimodal models are what happens when they share one space.

Anything a model must compare or feed in gets a vector: images, audio, users, code. Embedding models are their own product category (OpenAI, Cohere, open-weight BGE/E5) — ranked on MTEB-style retrieval benchmarks.

Embeddings

MapPlaybackNotes

Dense vectors that give tokens, images and users a geometry — looked up at input, searched at retrieval.

Statement

An embedding is a learned dense vector $\mathbf{e}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}$ that represents a discrete item — a token, image patch, user, product. Two facts make it a concept rather than a lookup table: (1) distances between vectors correlate with semantic similarity (measured by cosine), and (2) directions in the space correlate with semantic attributes — $\mathbf{e}_{king} - \mathbf{e}_{man} + \mathbf{e}_{woman} \approx \mathbf{e}_{queen}$.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

transformer-architecture — tokens enter as embeddings and leave as logits scored against embedding geometry (unembedding); without this step the transformer has no input. retrieval-augmented-generation — the entire index is an embedding space: retrieval is nearest-neighbour search over vector-search of these vectors. Same object, two jobs.

Teardown

  1. Lookup. Token id $i$ selects row $\mathbf{e}_i$ of a $|\mathcal{V}| \times d$ table — part of weights-theta, learned by gradient-descent like everything else.
  2. Geometry. cosine similarity turns "related" into a number; the famous word2vec analogies (king − man + woman ≈ queen, Mikolov et al. 2013) made the geometry legible.
  3. Three ways they're trained. (a) prediction — word2vec skip-gram; (b) contrastive-learning — CLIP pulls matching image/text pairs together; (c) in place — LLM embeddings learned end-to-end inside the stack, often tied to the output head.
  4. Beyond words. Users, products, images (contrastive-learning), code — anything a model must compare or feed in.
  5. The output side. The unembedding matrix $\mathbf{W}_U$ scores the final hidden state against vocabulary rows — often the same table transposed, which makes logits literal similarity scores.

Worked example

Two-dim toy space: king $(0.9, 0.8)$, queen $(0.85, 0.9)$, apple $(-0.7, 0.4)$.

  • $\cos(\text{king}, \text{queen}) = \frac{0.9 \cdot 0.85 + 0.8 \cdot 0.9} {\sqrt{0.9^2+0.8^2}\sqrt{0.85^2+0.9^2}} \approx \frac{1.485}{1.493} \approx 0.994$
  • $\cos(\text{king}, \text{apple}) \approx \frac{-0.31}{1.14} \approx -0.27$

The analogy: $\mathbf{e}_{king} - \mathbf{e}_{man} + \mathbf{e}_{woman}$ points near $\mathbf{e}_{queen}$ — vector arithmetic doing lexical work.

Open questions

  • Are the directions real axes of meaning or a projection artefact? — belongs to interpretability and probing.
  • Why do independently trained models learn nearly aligned spaces? — "platonic representation" hypothesis (Huh et al. 2024, arXiv:2405.07987).

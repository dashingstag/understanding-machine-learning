Document chunking

MapNotes

How documents are split before embedding — size, overlap, structure — the unglamorous step that decides what can be found.

Statement

How a corpus becomes embeddable units before indexing: split documents into chunks sized for the retriever (typically 200–1000 tokens) with overlap, respecting structure (headings, tables, code blocks) where possible. The atoms vector-search searches over — and the silent determinant of what retrieval-augmented-generation can find.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

retrieval-augmented-generation — a chunk is the unit of recall: split the answer across two chunks and it's unreachable; merge it into a wall of text and it's diluted. Chunking is upstream of everything, so its mistakes are unfixable downstream.

Teardown

  1. Size tradeoff. Small chunks → precise matches, missing context; large chunks → self-contained answers, muddy similarity. 300–500 tokens + 10–15% overlap is the default envelope.
  2. Structure-aware. Split at headings/paragraphs/code fences, keep tables whole, attach heading breadcrumbs ("§4.2 · Refunds · ...") — the single highest-leverage upgrade.
  3. Semantic/agentic chunking. Split where embeddings shift topic, or let an LLM draw boundaries — costs compute, helps at edges.
  4. Metadata. Source, date, section, permissions — chunks carry the fields the retriever filters on and the citation prints.
  5. Failure signature. A retrieval miss where the fact exists in the corpus is usually a chunking failure, not an embedder failure.

Worked example

Policy wiki, §4.2 refund table split at row 3 of 5:

  • Query "refund for annual plan?" → row 3's chunk says "annual: 50%" but its header context landed in the previous chunk → the retrieved fragment lacks the condition ("within 30 days") — answer wrong with perfect retrieval. Keep-tables-whole fixes it.

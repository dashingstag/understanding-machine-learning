Knowledge distillation

MapNotes

Training a small model to imitate a large one's outputs — how capability reaches cheap hardware.

Statement

Train a small "student" model to imitate a large "teacher": match the teacher's full output distribution (soft labels, kl-div to teacher logits-z softened by temperature) rather than the hard corpus labels. The student learns which wrong answers were close — richer signal per example — at ~2–5% of pretraining compute.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

inference-efficiency — most "small" production models are students: capability reaches cheap hardware by imitation, not by training small models on raw data. It's also the mechanism behind cheap model variants, speculative-decoding drafters, and some post-training pipelines (distilling a reasoning model's traces).

Teardown

  1. Soft targets. Hinton et al. 2015: teacher probabilities at high temperature carry "dark knowledge" — p(cat)=0.7, p(tiger)=0.2, p(car)=0.0001 encodes similarity structure hard labels lose.
  2. The loss. $KL(p_{teacher}^{\tau} \| p_{student}^{\tau})$ + optional ground-truth term — token-level for LMs: each position gets a full distribution to match.
  3. Sequence-level. Teacher generates the training set (Alpaca, Phi-style, R1-trace distillation) — the student SFTs on teacher outputs; simplest and the dominant modern form.
  4. Feature-level. Also match hidden states/attention maps — richer, but requires white-box teacher access.
  5. Legal/contest frontier. Distilling proprietary outputs sits in a ToS/grey zone — several 2024–25 "who distilled whom" debates hinge on trace provenance.

Worked example

Reasoning distillation (DeepSeek-R1 → 1.5B student):

  • Teacher generates 800k verified reasoning traces → student SFTs on them → small model outperforms same-size models trained on raw data by ~15–30 points on math — the "capability travels down" result in one number.

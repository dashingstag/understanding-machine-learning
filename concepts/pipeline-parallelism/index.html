Pipeline parallelism

MapNotes

Splitting layers across devices — and the pipeline-bubble scheduling problem it creates.

Statement

Assign contiguous layer ranges to different device groups and stream micro-batches through them like an assembly line — stage $i$ computes layers, ships activations to stage $i+1$. The idle time at fill/drain is the "pipeline bubble"; schedules (GPipe, 1F1B, interleaved) exist to shrink it. The layer-wise axis of distributed-training.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

distributed-training — when the model outgrows even a sharded node's tensor-parallel slice, layers must span machines. PP crosses inter-node links cheaply (activations only, at stage boundaries) at the price of scheduling complexity — it's what makes 100+ layer, trillion-parameter stacks resident at all.

Teardown

  1. Stage split. Layers divided evenly across $P$ stages; each stage holds only its slice of weights-theta + its activations.
  2. The bubble. A pipeline of depth $P$ with $M$ microbatches idles ~$(P-1)/(P+M-1)$ of the schedule — more microbatches → smaller bubble, more memory/queueing.
  3. Schedules. GPipe: fill, then drain (big bubble); 1F1B: steady alternating forward/backward (smaller); interleaved virtual stages: multiple smaller chunks per GPU — Megatron's production default.
  4. Communication. Only boundary activations cross stages — cheap; the real cost is imbalance and the first/last-stage load (embeddings, head).

Worked example

96 layers on 8 stages = 12 layers/stage, $M = 32$ microbatches:

  • GPipe bubble fraction ≈ $7/39 \approx 18\%$ of the step idle.
  • Interleaved (2 virtual stages): bubble halves to ~9% — same hardware, ~10% more real throughput, which is why it ships.

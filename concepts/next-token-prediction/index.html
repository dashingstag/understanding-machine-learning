Next-token prediction

MapNotes

The objective itself — one cross-entropy term per position is the entire training signal.

Statement

The pretraining objective: at every position $t$, the model's logits-z are softmaxed into a distribution and scored by cross-entropy against the true next token — $\mathcal{L} = -\sum_t \log p_\theta(x_{t+1} \mid x_{\le t})$. One term, trillions of times. perplexity-s ($e^{\mathcal{L}}$, see ppl) is the readable score: effective vocabulary size the model is still confused among.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

pretraining — this is the entire training signal; everything else is logistics. The surprising theorem of the field: a good enough density estimator of text becomes capable — "predict the next word" compiles grammar, facts and reasoning into weights-theta.

Teardown

  1. Self-supervision. The label is in the data — no humans annotate; the corpus is both input and answer key.
  2. Per-position supervision. One sequence of length $n$ yields $n$ training examples — the density that makes trillion-token corpora tractable.
  3. What it forces. To predict well, the model must learn syntax, then facts, then discourse structure, then (at the frontier) latent reasoning steps — the objective is capability-neutral but demanding.
  4. Teacher forcing. Training feeds true prefixes; generation feeds its own outputs — the exposure-bias gap behind compounding errors (post-training mitigates).
  5. Alternatives. Masked prediction (BERT), span corruption (T5), diffusion objectives (diffusion-models) — next-token won for generation + scale; the comparison is why LLMs are decoders.

Worked example

Sequence the cat sat → three supervised steps: given the predict cat; the cat → sat; the cat sat → next.

  • If $p(\text{cat}\mid\text{the}) = 0.3$: contribution $-\ln 0.3 \approx 1.20$.
  • $p(\text{sat}\mid\text{the cat}) = 0.6$: $-\ln 0.6 \approx 0.51$.
  • Mean loss ≈ 0.85 → perplexity $e^{0.85} \approx 2.3$ — the model is "choosing" among ~2.3 effective options.

RLHF (RL from human feedback)

MapNotes

The PPO-style reinforcement stage that made chat models usable — still the alignment workhorse.

Statement

Reinforcement learning that treats the reward-models score as the environment and the language model as the pi-policy: maximize rlhf-obj — expected reward minus kl-div to the frozen SFT policy, weighted by beta. Historically PPO; newer runs use GRPO-style group baselines.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

post-training — SFT imitates good answers; RLHF optimizes "preferred" directly — the difference that made chat assistants refuse, hedge and follow. It's also the canonical goodhart-law arena: optimize a proxy hard enough and it stops measuring what you meant.

Teardown

  1. The loop. Sample completions from pi-policy → score with RM → PPO/GRPO ascent with KL leash to the SFT anchor.
  2. The KL term. Without it the policy drifts to RM exploits and loses fluency — beta is the single dial between alignment and capability drift (the "alignment tax" knob).
  3. PPO. Actor–critic: value network estimates baselines, clipped updates keep steps safe — works, but 4 models in memory (policy, ref, RM, critic) is heavy.
  4. GRPO. Drop the critic: normalize reward within a group of samples per prompt — the DeepSeek-R1/verifiable-rewards default.
  5. Reward hacking. Over-optimization curve: true quality peaks then falls while RM score keeps rising (Gao et al. 2023) — the empirical Goodhart curve every run must watch.

Worked example

$\beta$ as the alignment dial (Gao et al., scaling laws for RM overoptimization): at $\beta$ too small, RM score ↑ 40% but human-rated quality ↓ after ~400 PPO steps — the policy writes longer, more confident answers the RM over-rewards. $\beta$ too large: no learning at all. The useful regime is a narrow middle.

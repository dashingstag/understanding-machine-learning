Reasoning and test-time compute

MapPlaybackNotes

Chain-of-thought, RL-trained long reasoning traces, and spending FLOPs at inference instead of training.

Statement

Models trained to produce long internal chain-of-thought traces — thousands of tokens of scratchpad before answering — and rewarded via RL on verifiable-rewards (correct math, passing tests). The result is a second scaling axis: compute-c spent at inference buys accuracy that parameter scale alone didn't reach. o1/R1-class systems are the existence proof.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

modern-machine-learning — the 2025–26 capability frontier runs on inference-time compute, not just bigger pretraining: "thinking" modes trade latency and token cost for accuracy on math, code and science — a new dial on scaling-laws and a new pricing axis in inference-efficiency.

Teardown

  1. CoT as scratchpad. chain-of-thought: intermediate steps externalize working memory — attention alone can't store intermediate products.
  2. From prompting to training. Wei et al. prompted it; o1/R1 trained it — RL on verifiable outcomes discovers which trace habits pay (backtracking, verification, decomposition).
  3. Verifiability as the gate. verifiable-rewards: only domains with cheap checkers (math, code, formal proofs) admit dense RL signal — the enabling condition and the current boundary.
  4. Test-time scaling laws. Accuracy vs reasoning-tokens is law-like (log-linear over ranges) — "think longer" is a real, purchasable axis.
  5. Cost and control. Traces cost tokens+time, leak into pricing; hidden-CoT debates (faithfulness — does the trace show the real computation?) feed interpretability and agents-and-tool-use.

Worked example

o1-style budget arithmetic (illustrative): GSM-style problem, direct answer ~60% for a strong base model; 500 reasoning tokens → ~85%; best- of-4 500-token traces → ~93%.

  • The same weights, no new pretraining — the delta is entirely spent flops at inference. The "reasoning scaling curve" is that accuracy- vs-tokens line.

Open questions

  • Verifiability frontier: extending dense rewards to non-checkable domains (writing, strategy, science) — rubric judges, process RMs or self-consistency?
  • Faithfulness: does the visible trace reflect the real computation? — Turpin et al. showed it often doesn't; safety stakes in interpretability.

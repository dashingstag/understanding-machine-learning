Multimodal models

One model over text, image, audio and video — and vision-language-action models for robots.

Statement

One model over text, image, audio and video: each modality is turned into embed-e-shaped input by modality-tokenizers, often aligned via contrastive-learning, then processed by the same transformer-architecture stack — with decoders (diffusion-models, token decoders) on the output side. Vision-language-action models extend the same recipe to robot control.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

modern-machine-learning — 2026-era products are natively multimodal: image understanding, voice, video and robotics all route through the same trunk. Understanding them needs the fusion story — how pixels become tokens — not just the text stack.

Teardown

  1. Tokenizing the world. modality-tokenizers: ViT patches, VQ codes, spectrogram slices — every modality becomes a sequence the trunk can attend to.
  2. Two fusion styles. Early fusion (native multimodal transformers — one stack eats mixed tokens) vs late fusion (CLIP-style encoders + an LLM via projector/adapter).
  3. Contrastive alignment. contrastive-learning: CLIP's dual encoder pulls matching image–text pairs together — the embedding space most open multimodal systems stand on.
  4. Generation vs understanding. Understanding reuses the LM head; generation needs modality decoders — diffusion for pixels (diffusion-models), codec decoders for audio.
  5. Embodiment. VLA models (RT-2, π0): same trunk, action tokens out — robotics is multimodality with motors attached.

Worked example

A "what's in this photo?" query, GPT-4V-class:

  • Image → ViT patches (~14×14 px each) → patch embeddings + learned projector → same d-model stream as text tokens.
  • The prompt's text tokens and image patch tokens share one attention block — cross-modal reasoning is just attention over a mixed sequence.
  • The model never "sees pixels" — it sees ~576 token-shaped vectors.

Open questions

  • Truly native token-free multimodality (pixels in, pixels out, one network) vs bolted-on encoders/decoders — which wins at scale?
  • Do modalities share an internal "meaning" representation — the platonic-hypothesis question from embeddings.

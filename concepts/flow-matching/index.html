Flow matching

MapNotes

Learning the velocity field between noise and data — the current formulation behind SD3/Flux-style generators.

Statement

Learn the velocity field that transports noise to data: define a probability path $x_t$ from Gaussian to data (typically linear interpolation $x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t\epsilon$), train $v_\theta(x_t, t)$ to predict the path's velocity $dx/dt$, then integrate the ODE from $t{=}1$ to $t{=}0$. The modern reformulation of diffusion-models — straighter paths, fewer steps, simpler objectives.

In my own words

One paragraph, as few symbols as possible. Written by me, not copied.

Why the parent needs it

diffusion-models — flow matching + diffusion transformers is the 2024+ default recipe (Stable Diffusion 3, Flux, Sora-class video). It explains why denoising works as transport, samples in fewer steps, and unifies DDPM-style noise schedules as special cases of path design.

Teardown

  1. Paths, not schedules. Define any interpolant $x_t$ between noise and data; the model learns its velocity field — DDPM is a curved special case; linear ("rectified flow") is the simple one.
  2. Conditional flow matching. Train on per-pair paths: pick $(x_0, \epsilon)$, form $x_t$, regress $v_\theta(x_t,t)$ onto $x_0 - \epsilon$ — tractable despite the marginal field being intractable.
  3. Sampling = ODE solve. Euler/Heun/Runge–Kutta from $t{=}1$ — straighter paths need fewer solver steps (the rectified-flow result: reflow makes paths straighter).
  4. Why it won. Deterministic, interpretable as transport theory, better at high guidance, compatible with distillation to few-step students — the practical recipe everywhere new models ship.

Worked example

Linear path $x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t\epsilon$:

  • Target velocity is constant: $dx/dt = \epsilon - x_0$ — the model learns to point from noise straight at data.
  • Euler with 20 steps on a straightened path ≈ DDIM with 100+ on the curved one — the fewer-steps claim in one line.
